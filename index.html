How selling software works

  1. You build or fork a product. It could be an app, a template or a download.

  2. A merchant of record sells it for you. Polar Software, Inc. takes the payment, charges the right sales tax or VAT in each country, sends the receipt and handles refunds. You don't need to register for tax in dozens of countries.

  3. Polar tells your app with a webhook. Your app checks the signature so nobody can fake a payment.

  4. Your app grants an entitlement. The customer gets access, a license or a download.

  5. You get paid out. Polar sends you the money minus its fees. The fee calculator shows how much you keep.

Glossary

MoR (merchant of record)
The company legally selling to the customer. It collects payment, handles tax/VAT, invoices, chargebacks and refunds. For Fork & Ship that's Polar Software, Inc.
Polar
A merchant-of-record platform for developers (polar.sh). It handles checkout, subscriptions, license keys and file downloads. It has a free sandbox for testing.
Webhook
An automatic HTTP message one service sends to another when something happens (e.g. "order paid"). It's signed with a secret so your app can check it's real.
Entitlement
What a customer is allowed to access after paying, such as repo access, a download or a feature. Your app stores it so it remembers who paid.
Sandbox
A test mode with fake money. Sandbox orders are never real revenue.
